## Decision: a successor of the Act 110 scope

Two shapes were considered:

- **(a)** a successor of the Act 110 scope that restores § 12-6-520 through the
  adapter;
- **(b)** a successor of the recovery scope that holds only the adapter's
  § 12-6-520 row.

This run builds **(a)**. It is the only shape that keeps the adapter's own output
and lets a cut swap cleanly. Two release checks decide it.

**The duplicate-citation check spans the release.** `_release_citation_paths`
(`src/axiom_corpus/corpus/release_quality.py:204-254`) keys every row of every
selected scope on `citation_path` alone. A second scope carrying the same path
raises `duplicate_release_citation` (`:243`). The check applies to every selector
with the `complete-expression-dates-v1` profile except `us-rulespec-2026-07-19`,
which is grandfathered by selector digest (`:25-27`). All sixteen Act 110 selectors
have the profile, and none is grandfathered.

**The parent check stays within one scope.** `_validate_provision_record` looks up
a row's parent only among its own scope's rows (`:835`). The comment at `:836-837`
says why: Supabase derives versioned parent UUIDs, so a parent in another scope
cannot satisfy the foreign key.

These checks rule out (b):

- The adapter's § 12-6-520 row has parent `us-sc/statute/title-12/chapter-6`.
- On its own, that row fails `missing_parent_citation`.
- Adding the title and chapter rows makes them collide with the Act 110 scope.
- The adapter always writes those container rows. It has `excluded_sections`, but no
  filter that keeps only named sections (`south_carolina.py:402-495`).
- Making (b) clean would mean shipping a row the adapter does not produce, with its
  parent cut off.

Under (a), the successor's paths are exactly the Act 110 scope's plus
`us-sc/statute/12-6-520`, and that path is in the recovery scope. A cut that drops
both predecessors and adds the successor therefore carries every path it carried
before, less the recovery scope's two junk block paths. No new duplicate can
appear. Keeping either predecessor beside the successor is a duplicate (see
[Release swap](#release-swap)).

### Act 110 does not touch § 12-6-520

The retained act, `session-laws/2026-act-110-h4216.html` (sha256 `9e3fbbd4…`), has
eight SECTIONs:

- SECTIONS 1-5 and 7 amend §§ 12-6-510(C), 12-6-50, 12-6-1140, 12-6-4910(1),
  12-6-1720(2)(a)(i) and 12-6-3632;
- SECTION 6 directs the department to adjust the withholding tables;
- SECTION 8 is the effective date.

The act mentions § 12-6-520 once, inside the new § 12-6-510(C)(1), which keeps the
new brackets "indexed in accordance with Section 12-6-520". The adapter's overlay
parser agrees: `parse_south_carolina_session_law_overlay(act, section="12-6-520")`
raises "session law does not amend South Carolina section 12-6-520"
(`south_carolina.py:935`). So no overlay applies, and the restored row is the
codified text. Its history ends at 2018 Act No. 266.

### Activation order

`scripts/check_release_gate.py` runs `_monotonicity_check` at activation. For each
`(jurisdiction, document_class)` pair a cut carries, it compares the incoming cut's
newest version with the active cut's (`_version_key`, `:71-79`: the date prefix,
then the full string). An older incoming version fails unless `--allow-regression`
is passed (`:372-379`). Equal versions fall back to publication time.

For `us-sc/statute`, that means three things:

- `2026-09-27-…` sorts after both predecessors, so a cut whose us-sc statute set is
  the swapped one passes against an active cut that still carries the July pair.
- Once a cut with the successor is active, any later cut whose newest us-sc statute
  version is one of the July pair fails. Every later cut must carry the successor or
  a newer SC statute scope.
- The w6 cut's newest us-sc statute versions are dated 2026-10-06. If it is active,
  a cut from an older lineage already regresses on this pair, whether or not that
  cut takes the swap. The swap belongs in the cut being activated, and in the w6
  lineage if that is the active one.

A `-r2026-09-27-…` suffix on the Act 110 version would also sort after it. The CLI
cannot produce that name, though: the adapter would append its title and chapter
suffix after the `-r` part, and renaming afterwards would mean re-stamping the
versioned ids.
