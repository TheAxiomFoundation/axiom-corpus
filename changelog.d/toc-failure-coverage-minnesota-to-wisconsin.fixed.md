The Minnesota, Nebraska, Washington, District of Columbia, Rhode Island, South Dakota, Utah and Wisconsin statute extractors now keep a container as an inventory gap with no provision when they can't fetch its table of contents, get back a maintenance, empty or wrong-shape page, or find no children listed. Coverage and strict release validation then report the run as incomplete, where before the extractor silently dropped every section under the container. In DC the same holds for a section file that the title index lists but the extractor can't find or parse. A gap for a page the extractor couldn't fetch cites the listing that names it as its source, and a gap for a page it read but couldn't use cites that page.

Containers that list nothing by law stay complete: Washington chapter pages that name their chapter and carry no sections; DC containers and titles that the index marks "[Reserved]"; Rhode Island containers, and South Dakota titles and chapters, whose caption, heading or catchline says reserved, repealed, transferred or superseded as a whole word; and Utah index pages with no child in force on the expression date.

In Rhode Island, South Dakota, Utah and Wisconsin, one failed table-of-contents page no longer aborts the whole run, though a failed section page still does in Rhode Island and Utah. In South Dakota, Utah and Wisconsin, an interrupt during a pooled fetch now stops the run as `KeyboardInterrupt` instead of surfacing as a `RuntimeError`. A run that writes no provisions still fails.
