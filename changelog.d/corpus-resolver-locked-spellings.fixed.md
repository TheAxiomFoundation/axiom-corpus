On a case-insensitive filesystem such as APFS, another spelling of a locked corpus path (`DATA/corpus/provisions/...` for `data/corpus/provisions/...`) now names the locked file before it is fetched as well as after. `load_provisions` and `load_source_inventory` fetch it, or refuse with fetching off, instead of reading an unfetched scope as empty; `require_materialized` refuses or fetches a partly fetched directory spelled that way and names the locked spelling in its fetch command; and `ensure_corpus_paths`, `resolve_corpus_path`, CLI inputs and `--base` (including corpus-wide reports' whole-tree check), and `corpus fetch --path` select its locked files. Whether two spellings share a place is decided against the filesystem without writing to it: by the entries' identity where the locked name exists, and below that by whether the deepest existing directory finds names in another case and the names are canonical caseless matches. On a case-sensitive filesystem the other spelling stays a separate path that no lock names. `fold_path` now lowercases an ASCII path directly, giving the same key as before; folding this repository's 63,855 locked paths went from about 1.6 s to under 0.01 s.
