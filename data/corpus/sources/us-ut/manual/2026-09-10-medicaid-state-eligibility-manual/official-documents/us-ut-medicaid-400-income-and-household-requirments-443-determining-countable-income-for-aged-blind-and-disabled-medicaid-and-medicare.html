Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

443 Determining Countable Income for Aged, Blind and Disabled Medicaid And Medicare Cost-Sharing Programs

Effective Date: June 1, 2020

Previous Policy (416 was renumbered to 443 as of June 1, 2020)

 

Introduction

These rules are for community Medicaid programs. These include:

·        Aged Medicaid

·        Blind Medicaid

·        Disabled Medicaid

·        Medicaid Work Incentive

·        Medicare Cost Sharing programs (QMB, SLMB, QI)

Use the rules in this section to determine countable income for individuals who do not receive SSI or who are not in an SSI-Protected group. See 329 to decide eligibility for an SSI recipient.

Use these rules to determine countable income for Medicare Cost-Sharing programs for individuals even if they receive SSI. For institutionalized individuals, use the rules for single adults to determine countable income for Medicare Cost-Sharing programs.

Determining countable income is different for a married couple, one eligible spouse in a married couple, a single adult or a child. The rules in A. and B. are for couples who live together.

For HCB waiver individuals, refer to the applicable waiver policy sections.

A.  Married Couple Both are Aged, Blind or Disabled

Use this income calculation when:

o      Both spouses are aged, blind or disabled.

·       Neither receives SSI,

·       Neither is in an SSI protected group,

·       Neither is applying for nor eligible for a HCB waiver, and

·       Both want Medicaid coverage.

o      Both spouses are eligible for Medicare and both want coverage on a Medicare Cost-Sharing program.

1.    Combine all unearned income of the household that is not excluded.

2.    Subtract the $20 disregard. This is the household’s countable unearned income.

3.    Total all earned income of the household and subtract any impairment related work expenses.

4.    If the unearned income was less than $20, subtract the remaining balance of the general disregard from the earned income.

5.    Calculate the ’$65 and 1/2 ‘earned income deduction (409-2). The result will be the countable earned income.

6.    Combine the unearned and earned income for the countable household income.

B.  One Spouse in a Married Couple

Use this income calculation when:

o      The individual is aged, blind or disabled and the spouse is not.

o      The aged, blind or disabled individual wants coverage and the spouse who is also aged, blind or disabled doesn’t want coverage

o      The individual is aged, blind or disabled and the spouse receives SSI.

o      Only one spouse has Medicare Part A.

o      Both spouses have Medicare Part A but only one spouse wants Medicare Cost-Sharing.

o      For MWI individuals, see 462-1 to determine how to count income from a spouse.

Do Not Use this calculation for a spouse who wants community Medicaid when the other spouse is eligible for a HCB waiver. See #3 Single Adult.

1.    Combine all unearned income of the individual that is not excluded with the spouse’s deemable unearned income (410-2).

2.    Subtract the $20 disregard. The remainder is the household’s countable unearned income.

3.    Subtract any impairment related work expenses from the individual’s gross earned income (409-2).

4.    Add the spouse’s deemable earned income to the remainder of #3 and subtract any remaining amount of the $20 disregard.

5.    Calculate the ‘$65 and ½ ‘ earned income deduction. The result will be the earned income for the household.

6.    Add the total from #2 to the total from #5. This is the household's countable income.

C.  Single Adult

Use the following calculation when the individual is:

o      Single

o      Age 18 and over, or otherwise emancipated

o      Married AND

·       has lived separately from the spouse for more than 1 month, or

·       the spouse is a resident of a medical institution, or

·       lives with a spouse who is eligible for a Medicaid HCB waiver.  

1.    Total all unearned income of the individual that is not excluded. This includes any contributions from a spouse in a nursing home or on a waiver.

2.    Subtract the $20 disregard. The remainder is the countable unearned income.

3.    Subtract any impairment related work expenses from the gross earned income.

4.    If the result of #2 was less than $20, subtract any remaining amount of the $20 income disregard from the earned income.

5.    Calculate the ‘$65 and 1/2’ deduction for the earned income. The result is the countable earned income.

6.    Combine the results of line #2 and line #5 for the person’s countable income.

D.  Child

Use the following calculation when the individual is an unmarried child under age 18. Use this calculation through the month the child turns 18. For the MWI, see 462-1 to see how to count income from parents.

1.    Total child’s unearned income that is not excluded.

2.    Add parents’ deemable income (410-3).

3.    Subtract the $20 general disregard. The remainder is the child’s countable unearned income.

4.    Subtract any impairment related expenses from the child’s gross earned income.

5.    Subtract any remaining amount of the $20 disregard.

6.    Calculate the ’$65 and 1/2 ‘ earned income deduction.

7.    Add the countable unearned income and earned income for total countable income.

* Do not deduct the $20 general income disregard from needs-based VA payments.
