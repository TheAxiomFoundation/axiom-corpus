Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

431-9.3 Deduct Less Than the SNA

Effective Date: November 1, 2021

Previous Policy (431-9.3 was previously found in section 409-11.3 until November 1, 2021)

 

When to Deduct Less than the Calculated SNA 

 

Sometimes the actual deduction from the member's income may be less than the calculated SNA. Only deduct the actual amount that is given to the community spouse.

 

1.    If the amount of the member's income remaining after deductions is less than the SNA, deduct the amount that is available.

 

Example 1: George is in a nursing home. His wife Gloria lives in their community home. The calculated SNA for Gloria is $1845.  However, George's total income is only $1760. He receives a $45 dollar personal needs allowance, leaving $1715. George only has $1715 left to give to Gloria. Deduct only $1715. 

2.    When the member gives less than the SNA to the community spouse, deduct only the amount the member gives.

 

Example 2:  Ginny and Lu are married. Ginny enters a nursing home. Lu has major medical issues and wants to receive Medicaid. Lu's monthly income is $859 a month. Ginny has $1500 a month in income.  Ginny only wants to contribute enough income to Lu to keep Lu's income just under the 100% FPL level for Aged Medicaid.  Determine how much income Lu can receive from Ginny, and deduct only that amount from Ginny's income.
