592-4 Designation of Burial Funds, Date of Exclusion, and Case Closure

Effective Date: April 1, 2015

Previous Policy

 

Some resources, such a burial contract the individual is making payments on, are clearly for the individual's burial because the contract will show whose burial the contract covers.  They do not need a separate designation since the intent is clear.  Other resources, however, must be designated as burial funds. The individual can designate other types of resources for burial such as a bank account as shown below.

 

An individual may designate burial funds by:

Indicating on the fund document (for example, the title on a bank account) that the fund is for the individual’s burial expenses, or

By signing a statement that shows:

The value and owner of the resources

For whom the burial funds are designated

The form(s) in which the resources are held (for example, pre-need funeral contract, bank account, life insurance policy, etc.), and

The date the individual first considered the funds set aside for burial.

 Date of intent

Accept the client’s statement of the date the individual first considered the funds set aside for burial unless you have evidence the funds were used and replaced after that date.

Effective date of exclusion

When you have established the date the individual considered the funds set aside for burial, then decide the first month for which the exclusion affects eligibility.

For Aged, Blind, Disabled, MWI, and Medicare Cost Sharing programs, the first month for which the exclusion affects eligibility is:

first of the month after the month in which the funds were separated from non-burial related funds and the individual considered the funds to have been set aside, or

the date of application if the individual considered the funds set aside and they were separated from non-burial related funds in the month before the date of application.

For Medically Needy Family, Child, and Pregnant Woman, the first month for which the exclusion affects eligibility is:

the date during the month on which the individual considered the funds set aside and had separated the funds from non-burial related funds, or

the date of application if the funds were considered set aside and were separated from non-burial related assets before the date of application.

Designated burial funds cannot be un-designated

Once an individual designates a burial fund, it remains a burial fund for up to one year after the case closes for one of the reasons shown below.  If the individual reapplies within 12 months of the closure date, and the funds have not been used for another purpose, the burial fund exclusion still applies.  However, if the individual has acquired any other burial assets, the amount of funds that can be excluded may be reduced.  Such closures include:

closure for not completing a review or returning a verification,

closure for not meeting a spenddown or MWI premium,

closure for being over the applicable income limit for a program,

closure for being over the asset limit,

closure for being in a public institution,

closure for address unknown, and

other similar causes.  

If the individual does not reapply within 12 months after the closure, the burial fund designation is lost.  The individual can make a new designation of burial funds upon application, but the excluded amount is limited to $1500.  Accumulated interest above $1500 will be a countable resource.

The burial fund exclusion is lost and burial funds must be redesignated as if they had not existed, if the case closes because the individual:

is no longer disabled, or

is no longer a child, or

no longer has a dependent child for family-related coverage, or

is no longer a pregnant woman, AND

does not meet another category for Medicaid eligibility.

However, if one family member no longer meets criteria for Medicaid, but is still deemed in the household with eligible household members, continue to exclude the burial funds for the ineligible family member when deeming assets from him or her.

After a case closure for one of the reasons listed in B. above, excluded interest that was earned on the previously excluded funds, becomes countable and the $1500 exclusion limit would apply to the new designation of burial funds upon reapplication for Medicaid.

If the individual dies, and other family members in the household are still receiving Medicaid, burial funds designated for the deceased individual may become a countable asset for the other family members to the extent they are not used for the deceased individual's burial or funeral expenses.  Determine if the funds were used for the burial expenses and if any must count as an asset.

If the individual uses the funds for another purpose the burial fund exclusion may require a re-designation.  The funds used for another purpose are no longer burial funds.  (See 592-9)
