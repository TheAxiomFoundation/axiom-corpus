Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

521-42 Payments Under the Individual Indian Accounts Settlement Litigation Approved by Pub. L. 111-291

Effective Date: May 1, 2011

No Previous Policy

  1. Individual Indian Accounts Settlement

On December 8, 2010, a law was signed that authorizes the settlement of a law suit concerning Individual Indian Accounts.  The law requires the federal government to reimburse amounts to individual Indians who have or had Individual Indian accounts to account for inadequate account of funds.  

The law also establishes a trust land consolidation fund to buy individual interests in trust or restricted lands that will then be conveyed back to the tribes.  The land trust will make payments to individual Indians who hold ownership interests in lands that individuals cannot sell or use because of the multiple ownership or fractional interests.  Payments will be based on the market value of these lands.  The purchase of such lands will occur between 2011 and 2021.

  1. Treatment of Payments

Payments received under the Individual Indian Accounts Settlement Litigation Act are excluded from countable resources for one year after the date of receipt.  An individual may receive one or more of the following payments.

  1. Individual Indians who have or who had IIM accounts during the years covered by this law suit will receive an initial payment of $1000.  They may receive subsequent payments based on a prorated amount of the available funds.  

  2. Individual Indians who have interest in lands that the land trust identifies to purchase will receive a payment for their interest in those lands.  The amount of payments will be based on market values, and the timing of payments will depend on when the land trust is able to identify the lands to purchase.

  3. The land trust will also establish an educational scholarship fund.  Individual Indians may receive scholarships from the fund for educational costs.

For income exclusion of these payments, see Section 417-14.
