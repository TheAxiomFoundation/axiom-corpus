431-9.4 Hearing Officer's SNA Budget Sheet Instructions

 

Instructions for Hearings Officers for

Requests to increase a Spousal Needs Allowance

Revised 5/2018

 

When a community spouse asks for a hearing to increase the spousal needs allowance, send the Community Spouse Budget Sheet and instructions for completing it to the community spouse. The Budged Sheet tells the spouse to send a copy to the Adjudication Office. If actual receipts are needed, the agency needs to send a written request for receipts.

 

A hearing needs to be scheduled to occur within 30 days of the request. Include copies of the completed worksheet, the budget sheet from the spouse if provided, or any receipts with the hearing decision.

 

Hearings Officers need to apply the following protocol for evaluating expenses.

 

1.    Evaluate all of the community spouse’s expenses. When a community spouse claims the Spousal Needs Allowance derived using the formula in Medicaid policy is insufficient, we need to review all of the individual’s expenses to determine if they warrant an increase. This can only be done if we include all of the types of expenses an individual in the community has. Use the worksheet from the spouse and these directions to help identify expenses so decisions can be consistent and fair for all requests. Help the spouse identify expenses he or she has.

2.    Expenses that must be considered include the following:

·         Shelter costs (mortgage or rent, property tax, home or renter’s insurance, HOA or Community Coop fees.) Prorate annual expenses to arrive at monthly cost.

·         Utilities (use the Utility Allowance from Food Stamp program based on what utilities the member pays for)

·         Home maintenance and repairs, yard care and snow removal expenses (allow actual expenses such as yard care or snow removal. Must make some allowance for maintenance/repairs if the person owns a home)

·         Vehicle expenses like a car payment, automobile insurance, licensing, gas, allowance for routine maintenance, oil changes, repairs (estimate costs for routine care like oil changes or service checks)

·         Other transportation costs if person does not own a car (i.e.: monthly bus pass, taxi expenses, gas if someone else provides transportation—in some cases it may be appropriate to allow some amount for each of these types of transportation. Note: UTA monthly pass that includes Frontrunner is $99 in 2018 for someone age 65+; it is $198 for someone under age 65.)

·         Health insurance premiums; Medicare premiums applicable to the community spouse, such as Part B and D, or a Supplemental

·         Medical, dental and eye care. Estimate what someone needs for the year to arrive at an annual amount—include yearly deductibles (Medicare has annual deductibles), average copayments/ coinsurance, private pay, hospital care, durable medical supplies, other medical care supplies, dental and vision care, etc. (Must allow some reasonable amount each month)

·         Payments for any past medical or dental expenses the spouse is still paying off. (If any bills are for the institutionalized spouse, and they are still owed to the provider, they need to be deducted directly from the institutionalized spouse’s income. The agency must address those expenses if they have not been allowed before.)

·         Prescription and over-the-counter medications

·         Food allowance (use the maximum Food Stamp allowance for one person)

·         Special dietary foods

·         Non-food items such as cleaning & laundry supplies, paper goods, etc. (at least $40/month)

·         Personal care supplies (at least $40/month)

·         Clothing/shoes (at least $40/month)

·         Hair care (at least $40/month)

·         Hobbies, fitness activities, TV/Internet, pet care (food, vet care, grooming identified by spouse), Newspaper/Magazines

·         Allow payments for funeral/burial arrangements the couple is buying, if they do not have prepaid funeral plans. (This could be payments to a mortuary or on a life insurance plan if that is their only arrangement for their funerals.)

·         Review other expenses a spouse claims to determine a reasonable allowance. The spouse may have used credit cards or a loan to pay for expenses such as medical bills, home or car repairs, legal bills, or similar things.

·         Do not allow expenses for charitable contributions or expenses of someone who is not a dependent of the spouse.

3.    Be consistent from one case to another for certain expenses. Most of the items on the worksheet are expected expenses and require some allowance.

4.    When making changes to the amount of an expense the spouse lists, explain why the amount is being changed. For example, spouse says she pays $500 on a credit card bill each month. Find out what she is paying off (i.e.: I had my roof repaired; I paid for my granddaughter’s wedding dress). The Hearings Officer should use discretion in making a reasonable allowance based on the cause of the debt.

5.    The Excel spreadsheet has formulas to add the expenses from each category, and to sum up at the bottom. Add the spouse's monthly income in the appropriate box. The spreadsheet will deduct the amount from the total expenses to arrive at the Spousal Needs Allowance.
