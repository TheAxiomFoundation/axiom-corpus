531-2 SSI Lump Sums for Retroactive Benefits for a Minor Child

Effective Date: September 1, 2004

No Previous Policy

 

When a minor child will receive an SSI lump sum payment for retroactive months’ benefits in excess of 6 month's worth of benefits, SSI will require the child’s representative payee to establish a “dedicated bank account” to receive and maintain such payments.  The payee may also deposit subsequent SSI lump sum payments that equal or exceed one month’s benefits into the dedicated account.  The dedicated account will be an excluded resource for Medicaid purposes as long as the account is maintained according to SSI’s requirements for such accounts.  In addition, income earned on these funds is excluded from income.

The funds in these dedicated accounts can only be used for specific expenses related to the impairment of the child.  The funds cannot be used to reimburse the cost of support and maintenance of the SSI recipient.  The allowed uses are as follows:

  1. Education or job skill training;

  2. Personal needs assistance;

  3. Special equipment or housing modifications;

  4. Medical treatment, therapy or rehabilitation; or

  5. Other items or services which SSA determines are appropriate.

The representative payee will be required to report to SSA on the use of these funds.  SSI can recover benefits from the payee that are misused.

When the child turns 18, if he or she continues to receive SSI benefits, the restrictions for the dedicated bank account still apply, even if the individual no longer has a representative payee.  Continue to exempt the dedicated account from resources.

If an individual with a dedicated bank account is no longer receiving an SSI payment, the dedicated bank account becomes a countable asset the month after the person becomes ineligible for SSI.  However, for children who occasionally do not receive an SSI payment because the parents receive an extra paycheck in a month, the dedicated bank account can still be considered an exempt resource in the month that the child does not receive an SSI payment.  The child is still “eligible” but income exceeds the payment level. 

The exemption is lost only if the child becomes ineligible to receive any more SSI payments (for instance, other countable resources exceed the SSI resource limit, or the child is no longer considered disabled.)
