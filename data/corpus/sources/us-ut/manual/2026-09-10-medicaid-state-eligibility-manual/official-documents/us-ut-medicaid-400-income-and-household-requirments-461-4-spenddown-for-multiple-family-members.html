461-4 Spenddown for Multiple Family Members

Effective Date: June 1, 2020

No previous policy 

(Sections in 415 have been rewritten into 461 sections as of June 1, 2020)

A.   Multiple Family Members with Medicaid Spenddowns

When determining eligibility for different members of a family, it is possible to arrive at different spenddown amounts for different people. This is because of differences in deciding income and household size for different programs. If more than one person in the family wants medically needy coverage, the following rules apply.

 

1.    When different household members have different spenddown amounts, and multiple family members want coverage, meeting the largest spenddown meets all other spenddowns.

·        If not everyone with a spenddown wants coverage, collect the highest spenddown of those who want coverage. This will cover any lower spenddowns for other family members.

2.    If a family member chooses to spend down with cash, all family members receiving Medicaid must make the same decision. Apply any health insurance premiums first. If there are medical deductions that can be used, apply those to reduce or meet the spenddown. Medical bills used to meet a spenddown reduces the spenddown for other related household members. If a case has two unmarried parents of a child, there are some additional considerations. See #3.

·        Exception:  If one family member has already paid cash to meet his or her spenddown, and later a different family member applies for Medicaid for that month, that family member may use incurred medical bills.  The cash payment already made will not reduce the other family member's spenddown. Do not refund the cash payment. The family would be meeting two separate spenddowns for the same month.

·        Example:  Husband receives Disability Medicaid and paid a $200 cash spenddown to be eligible in May. Later in May, his wife has a medical emergency and hospital stay. She applies for Medicaid for May and has a spenddown. She can use her incurred medical bills to meet her spenddown, but the cash payment her husband made will not reduce her spenddown.

3.       For different case numbers with family members in common, count the same medical expenses for each case.

a.    You can only apply bills to meet a medical spenddown when the individual, the spouse or the parent has a legal obligation to pay the expenses.

·        Examples:

o If unmarried parents have a child in common, they are both included in the child's household for medically needy coverage.

o If the child has a spenddown, the spenddown can be met with cash, or with medical bills of the child, the child's siblings and the parents.  

o The medical expenses of each unmarried parent can be used to meet the spenddown for their child-in-common.

o The parents must be on different cases if they want coverage. If the child has medical bills that can be used to meet a spenddown, the bills can be used to meet each parent's spenddown, as well as the child's spenddown. This is because each parent is responsible for the bills.

o Do not allow medical bills for non-family members, such as a live-in boyfriend or girlfriend. For example, the parent of a dependent child is not legally responsible to pay a live-in girlfriend's medical expenses.  If she is not the mother of the dependent child, her medical expenses cannot be used to meet the spenddown of the child, or of the child's parent.

b.       Subject to the policy on bills of unrelated household members, once the higher spenddown has been met for someone included in more than one medically needy program household, that spenddown covers the spenddown for other household members who have a lower spenddown. This applies even if a household member is included in two different cases.

 

B.   Family Members with Spenddowns and MWI premiums

When a family member owes a Medicaid spenddown and another family member owes a MWI premium, find out if they want to meet both the spenddown and the MWI premium. If they want to meet both, find out if they have medical bills they could use to meet the spenddown.

1.    Apply health insurance premiums and medical deductions toward the spenddown before accepting cash for the MWI premium.  (431-1.1 and 431-1.2)

2.    If there is a remaining spenddown, find out if the member has incurred bills to use. Incurred bills cannot be combined with cash. Apply incurred bills to meet the rest of the spenddown if the member wants them applied. Once the full spenddown has been met, the member can then pay the MWI premium with cash. [In this case, the MWI premium payment does not reduce the spenddown of the other family member.]

3.    If the member does not want to use incurred bills, the member can pay the MWI premium in cash. This will further reduce the spenddown owed. Any remaining spenddown can be met with cash or other medical deductions if not previously applied.

4.    If a family member is eligible for a Home and Community Based waiver, other rules apply. (463)
