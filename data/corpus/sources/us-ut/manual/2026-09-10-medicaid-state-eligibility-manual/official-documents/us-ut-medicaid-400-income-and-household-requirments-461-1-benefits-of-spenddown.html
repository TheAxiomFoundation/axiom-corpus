Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

461-1 Benefits of Spenddown

Effective Date: June 1, 2020

 

No previous policy

(Sections in 415 have been rewritten into 461 sections as of June 1, 2020)

 

Individuals not otherwise eligible for Medicaid may benefit by meeting a spenddown to receive coverage. They should only meet a spenddown when the coverage it is cost effective.

 

A.   Benefits of Meeting a Spenddown

 

The following are examples of when meeting a spenddown may benefit a individual.

1.    The total owed on medical expenses that Medicaid could cover (including the Medicare Part B premium) exceeds the amount of the spenddown. 

2.    The member is Medicare eligible and by meeting a spenddown, they receive improved benefits under their Medicare Part D plan. If they meet a spenddown between January and June, they have reduced Part D costs through the rest of the year.  Meeting a spenddown in at least one month between July and December lowers their Part D costs through the rest of the year as well as through the following year.

3.    The member owes medical bills Medicaid will not pay. Using those bills can allow a member to receive Medicaid by reducing or meeting spenddown in a retroactive month or a current month. This way, Medicaid can cover the member's medical expenses in such months and save the member money.

4.    The member has paid health insurance premiums and those premiums meet or exceed the spenddown. Premiums may include health insurance or Medicare premiums from the retroactive period. Thus, the member can receive Medicaid to help pay for current medical expenses. The member may also use a combination of health insurance premiums, allowable medical expenses and cash to meet the spenddown. 431-1.1

 

B.   Eligibility for Spenddown Medical Programs

 

Only certain non-MAGI based programs allow an individual to meet a spenddown to become eligible.

 

1.    The Medically Needy categories include:

a.    Families (i.e., parents and caretaker relatives) (345)

b.    Children (347)

c.     Pregnant Women (349)

d.    Aged, Blind or Disabled Persons (303)

e.    Certain individuals eligible for home and community based services waivers with income above a set amount (384-2, 386-2 and 387 )

 

2.    The following general criteria applies to medically needy Medicaid.

 

a.    Meet specific non-financial criteria such as being disabled or being a family with dependent children

b.    Meet the applicable asset limit (503)

c.     Spend down their excess income to an income limit called the Basic Maintenance Standard (BMS) for family, child and pregnant woman programs with medical bills or cash.

d.    Spend down excess income to 100% of the poverty level for aged, blind or disabled individuals with medical bills or cash.  (Table VII)
