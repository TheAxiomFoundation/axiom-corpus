Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

425-4 Deeming for Long-Term Care Programs

Effective Date: November 1, 2021

No Previous Policy

 

Introduction

For individuals in need Medicaid for long-term care services such as in a nursing home or under a community based waiver, deeming will depend on the program and the category of coverage for which the individual qualifies.

 

A.   Nursing Home Residents

1.    When an individual is a resident in a medical institution such as a nursing home, and meets eligibility as an aged, blind or disabled person, do not deem income from the individual's spouse or parent.

·       Spousal deeming stops the month of entry if the individual will be in the nursing home for at least 30 consecutive days.

     ·      If the individual has a spouse living in a community setting, follow the Spousal Impoverishment rules in 431-9.

·       Deeming from a parent stops the month after a child enters a nursing home, and restarts the month the child returns home. (For a newborn child, see #3.)

 

2.    If a minor child eligible under the poverty level Child Medicaid program is only residing in a medical institution temporarily, the child is considered temporarily absent from the parent's home.  Continue to determine the child's eligibility under the Child Poverty group as if the child lived in the home with the parent.

 

3.    Babies born in a hospital.

Refer to policy in 215-4. In the case of a baby born in a hospital who meets disability criteria, do not deem parents' income to the infant until the month after the child leaves the medical institution and starts living in the parents' home.

 

4.    A disabled child who enters a medical institution from the parents' home, becomes a resident of an institution the month after leaving the parents' home. The child should be opened under a nursing home program type. Deeming from the parents stops the month after the child enters the nursing home.

·       Exception:  If the child enters a nursing home one month and returns home by the end of the following month, the child does not become a resident of the nursing home. Parental deeming does not stop in this situation.

 

B.   Home and Community Based Waivers

Eligibility and deeming varies depending on the HCB waiver for which the individual qualifies.

In general, though, the month an individual is approved for an HCB waiver program, deeming from the individual's spouse or parent ends. Current policy for HCB waivers prohibits deeming income from a spouse or parent.

Refer to policies in the 380's sections for the specific waiver policy on counting income.
