Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

460-4 Changes in Payments, Refunds and Fair Hearings

Effective Date: June 1, 2020

No previous policy 

(Sections in 415 have been rewritten into 460 sections as of June 1, 2020)

 

A.   Changes and Refunds Affecting Previous Months

Sometimes a member may have a change affecting a previous month that requires a redetermination of eligibility.  The eligibility agency may need to redetermine eligibility if the change could affect the current month or one of the 3 prior months.

1.    No payment or reduced payment.  If the member was determined eligible with a payment but that payment has not been met, gather the needed verification to determine if eligibility in the current month or one of the 3 previous months changed.  Adjust eligibility, and the payments, if applicable, as follows:

 

a.    If the change causes the member to qualify without a payment, approve eligibility and notify the member.

b.    If the change causes the payment(s) to go down, recalculate the new payment. Send a notice to the member with the new payment amount for each affected month.

                                    i.        The notice must explain how to meet the payment and allow the member 30 days to meet the payment. (460-2)

                                   ii.         If the member has provided medical bills that could now meet the reduced payment, verify what month or months the member wants coverage for before applying the available medical bills. If the member wants coverage, apply the bills to meet the spenddown.

                                  iii.         For the current month (month change reported), the member may have already met the spenddown. In this case, request a refund, or adjust the bills applied (as applicable.)

b.    If the member met the previous month's spenddown with a cash (or partial cash) payment, the eligibility agency must refund the difference to the member. The eligibility agency must do the refund automatically; the member does not have to request the refund.

c.     If both cash and expenses were used, decide if the cash paid is less than the difference in the spenddown.  Request a refund and adjust the medical bills to make up the remaining difference in spenddowns.

Example:  Jill paid a $400 spenddown for May, using $300 in bills and $100 in cash. In June she reports receiving less income in May because she was hospitalized and did not work as much. The spenddown owed for May is now $250.  Refund the $100 cash and adjust the applied bills by $50.

d.    If the member met the spenddown with medical expenses (deductions or incurred expenses), the agency must adjust the bills applied equal to the difference in the spenddown met.

e.    Remove or adjust the amount of incurred bills first as they could now be paid by Medicaid.

 

2.    Increased payment.

a.    Payment met.  If the change causes the member to have a higher payment, and the member has already met the payment, do not adjust the payment. The agency cannot increase a payment after the member has met it.  An overpayment referral may be needed in the case of an untimely change report.

b.    Payment due in future.  If the member has not meet the payment for such previous month, and the due date for the payment is in the future, do not adjust eligibility. The agency has provided a decision to the member. The member has the right to meet that spenddown.

c.     Payment due date has passed. If the due date for the payment has passed, the member has not met that payment, and the change affects one of the 3 prior months, redetermine eligibility and the new payment amount, as applicable. Treat the change report like a new application request. Notify the member of the change to eligibility, and the new deadline for meeting the payment (as applicable.)

 

3.    Member requests a refund.  Members may request a refund of a cash payment as follows.

a.    A member pays an allowable medical deduction or a health insurance premium during the month, but the amount was not applied toward the spenddown before the member paid a cash spenddown.  

·        Decide if the medical expense is an allowed deduction that has not been applied before.

·        If the member paid a health insurance premium, make sure it has not been applied before. )

·        If the expense meets the criteria for an incurred expense, the member cannot receive a refund after the agency issues Medicaid eligibility.  This is because Medicaid will cover incurred expenses after issuing eligibility.

·   If the expense was a deduction or a health insurance premium, see section 825-1.3 for policy on correction of overstated liability errors.

 

b.    The member believes the spenddown or cost-of-care they paid was more than the cost of services they received in the month. If they paid cash, it is possible to receive a refund.

·        The cost of services includes payments to ACOs and contracted mental health services. In some cases, this may exceed the payment the member made.

·        These refunds can take over 12 months to process to allow providers to bill for services.

·        If the member used medical bills, change what bills were applied to cover the payment, removing incurred bills first. That way Medicaid can pay more of the member's bills.

 

4.    Refunds due to Agency Error. Sometimes the agency may calculate an incorrect payment. This may be due to entering the wrong income or household information, counting exempt income, failure to allow certain deductions, etc.

a.    When an error is discovered that reduces the payment, redetermine eligibility and recalculate the payment (as applicable.)

b.    If the payment is eliminated or reduced, initiate a refund of any cash payment the member made. The member does not have to request a refund; the agency is responsible to initiate it. These refunds do not take 12 months to process.

c.     If the member used medical expenses to meet the spenddown, modify the applied bills (incurred first), so Medicaid can pay more of the member's bills. [NOTE: if incurred bills were used to meet a spenddown or cost of care, there will not be a cash refund, as cash is not supposed to be used in the same month as incurred bills.] "Unapplied" deductions may be available to use in a future month.

d.    If the member is a nursing home resident, reassess eligibility and notify the member and nursing home of the reduced cost of care contribution.  The nursing home must refund the member. Workers must notify the Dept. of Health Medicaid Claims staff of the change so they can adjust the nursing home payment.

 

5.    MWI Premiums and refunds. The MWI program does not allow refunds when the member believes the services received were less than the premium. This is because the MWI premium payment is based on a percentage of income. It is not associated with the actual cost of services received. The only time an MWI payment can be refunded is when the agency made an error in the calculation, and charged more than what was allowed.

 

 

B.   Fair Hearings/Appeals

When a case goes to a fair hearing or an appeal, take appropriate actions based on the final decision.  

1.    If the hearing decision is that the member was eligible for Medicaid, determine if there is a payment owed for any of the months in question.

2.   The agency must notify the member with the payment information.  (443-2)

3.    Members have 30 days from the mailing date of the notice to meet payment for the benefit months being approved or changed. If the 30th day is a non-business day, the member has until the first business day following the 30th day to meet the payment.
