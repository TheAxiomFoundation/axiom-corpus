Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

410 Deeming Income for Aged, Blind and Disabled Medicaid and Medicare Cost Sharing Programs

Effective Date: November 1, 2021

Previous Policy (410 were previously found in 427 until November 1, 2021)

 

 

A.   Introduction

 

Deeming refers to a process of deciding the amount of income or assets from an ineligible spouse, or an ineligible parent of a minor child, to count as available to the eligible person. For a married couple, it can also play a role in deciding what household size to use for the program income test. (For asset deeming, see 503-1)

 

Spouses are financially responsible for each other, and parents are financially responsible for their children. Some of the income from a spouse or parent counts as income available to the eligible spouse or eligible child. The deeming process includes certain allocations for a spouse or parent to provide support to other family members and themselves. Deemed income is added to the eligible individual's income to determine eligibility for medical assistance.  

 

B.   Definitions

 

1.    Allocation:  An allocation is an amount deducted from income that is being deemed to use for the support of certain persons.

2.    Disregard:  A disregard is a deduction from income to determine the countable income.

3.    Eligible child:  An eligible child is a biological or adopted child who is blind or disabled, under age 18, not married and living with one or both parents or is temporarily absent. (408-6) The child is under age 18 until the month after the month in which the child turns age 18.

4.    Ineligible child: An ineligible child is a biological or adopted child of the eligible person or spouse, or a sibling of an eligible child. An ineligible child must be:

a.    Unmarried;

b.    Under age 18, or under age 22 and a student; and

c.    Living in the household of the eligible individual or temporarily absent.

5.    Ineligible spouse or parent: An ineligible spouse or parent is:

a.    A spouse or parent who is not aged, blind or disabled;

b.    A spouse who is aged, blind or disabled, does not receive SSI, and does not want coverage for Medicaid;

c.    A parent who is aged, blind or disabled and does not receive SSI;

d.    A spouse who does not qualify for Part A Medicare or does not want coverage for a Medicare Cost-Sharing program, if determining eligibility for a Medicare Cost Sharing program.

6.    Public Income Maintenance (PIM) payment:  This refers to income payments that are needs-based. It includes SSI, FEP, General Assistance, needs-based VA benefits, Refugee Cash Assistance, Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA) General Assistance, and Federal Disaster Relief Assistance.

7.    Student:  A student is a person under age 22 who is regularly attending school, college or training to prepare for a paying job.

 

C.   General Deeming Policy

 

When deciding whether to deem, the following rules apply.

1.    The eligible person must live with the ineligible spouse or ineligible parent, or be temporarily absent from the home.

·       A person is temporarily absent if the person intends to, and does return in the same month or the following month. Deeming does not stop in this case.

·       A child away at school or vocational training is temporarily absent if the child is still under parental control and returns home on some weekends, holidays or vacations.

o    Continue deeming to the eligible child while away at school;

o    Continue to allow an allocation for an ineligible child meeting the age and student criteria.

o    Stop allowing an allocation for an ineligible child the month after the child exceeds the age limit, or the month after the child is no longer a student (or does not intend to return to school for the following regular term.

Note: a student who intends to return in the fall is still considered a student, even if they do not take summer classes.)

·       A child living in a private, nonmedical facility that does not meet the criteria of an educational/vocational training facility is not a resident in the parent's home.

o    Deeming income from the parents ends the month after the child becomes a resident in a private, non-medical institution that is not for educational/vocational training.

o    Brief visits home (less than one full calendar month) do not change the child's residency at the facility, so no deeming to that child will occur during brief visits to the parents' home.

·       A parent or spouse who is absent due to an active duty military assignment is temporarily absent.

o    Continue deeming income and assets during the temporary absence.

o    Stop deeming the month after a spouse or parent leaves for military services if they do not intend to return when the military service ends.

o    If the absent family member initially intends to return, but later changes their intent to return, stop deeming the month after the person's intent to return changes.

o    Accept the client's statement or a statement from the ineligible spouse or parent.

2.    Deeming applies each month the eligible person is not receiving an SSI payment or is not 1619b eligible, and the spouse or parent has income subject to deeming.

3.   An ineligible spouse's income is deemed only when it exceeds the Maximum Allocation for a Spouse. (Table II)

4.    Do not allow an allocation for a parent or an ineligible child who receives a PIM payment.

5.    If an eligible child lives with more than two parents, for example the child lives with both biological parents and the spouse of one of those parents, deem from all of the parents (including the step-parent.)
