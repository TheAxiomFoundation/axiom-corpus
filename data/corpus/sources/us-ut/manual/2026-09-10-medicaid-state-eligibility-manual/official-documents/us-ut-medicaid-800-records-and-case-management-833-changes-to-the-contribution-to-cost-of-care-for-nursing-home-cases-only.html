Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

 

833 Changes to the Contribution to Cost of Care for Nursing Home Cases Only

Effective Date: July 1, 2009

Contact DHHS Policy Specialist for Previous Policy

 

The 10*10*10* rule applies to nursing home cases.  This rule says that a Medicaid recipient must report a change within 10 calendar days from the date of the change, the agency has 10 calendar days to take action on the reported change, and the agency must provide a 10 day advance notice of a negative action. Advance notice is not required to make a change that benefits the member.

The nursing home member or their representative is responsible to report any change in income or circumstances within 10 days of the date of the change.  (See Section 107-4 for a description of changes that must be reported.)

A report of a change or other information may be received from a source other than the recipient.  Any reported change or information received by the agency must be evaluated to determine if it could affect eligibility.

A member has reported the change on time if the report is received by the agency by the close of business  on the 10th day after the member learns of the change.  If the 10th day falls on a non-business day, the member has until the close of business on the first business day after the 10th day.  This same requirement applies to the due date for verifications to be returned.

Workers must request any necessary verification within 10 days of the date a change is reported or information is received.  

 

INCOME CHANGES

If a change results in an increase in the members contribution to the cost of care and the agency can take action within 10 days AND can give 10 day notice, the change can be effective for the next month.

If 10 day notice cannot be provided, the change will be effective the following month.

If an income change results in case closure, determine if the member would be eligible under any other Medicaid programs.  A new application is not required.  However, all eligibility   requirements for the new program still apply.

 

LUMP SUM PAYMENTS

Count the net lump sum payment as income for the month it is received.  Any amount remaining after the end of that month is considered an asset.  See section 825 for policy on medical assistance payment errors.

 

ON GOING MONTHLY INCOME

The nursing home will inform the worker when the member's income exceeds the private pay rate.  They can also tell the worker what the member's private pay rate is, what the member's actual Medicaid rate would be, and what the anticipated ancillary charges will be for the member.

If the member's contribution to the cost of care is more than the Medicaid rate, but less than the private pay rate, the member is still eligible for Nursing Home Medicaid.

If the member's contribution to the cost of care is more than the private pay rate, they may still be eligible for Ancillary Nursing Home.      

It needs to be determined if it is in the member's best interest to continue Medicaid and pay the spenddown for Ancillary services OR if it would be better for the member to pay the private pay rate and ancillary charges on his own.

 

DISCHARGE FROM A MEDICAL INSTITUTION

When a member is single or married to a resident of a medical institution or someone receiving waiver services, the member is NOT considered to be a resident of a medical institution for the month of discharge.  It will be necessary to redetermine eligibility based on the community Medicaid rules or the applicable Waiver rules.

When notified of the death of a member who is a resident of a medical institution, close the case.  The effective date of the closure must be the same as the date the member died.

If the member has a spouse who is not on waiver services or a resident of a medical institution, the member is considered a resident of the institution in the month of discharge.  No liability adjustment is necessary for the month of discharge.  The member may request a refund if he believes the actual cost of medical services was less than the liability paid.
