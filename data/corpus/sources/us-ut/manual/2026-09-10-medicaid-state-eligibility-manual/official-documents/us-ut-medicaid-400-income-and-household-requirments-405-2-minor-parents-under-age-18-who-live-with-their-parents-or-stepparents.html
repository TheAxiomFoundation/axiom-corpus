Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

405-2 Minor Parents Under Age 18 Who Live with Their Parents or Stepparents

Effective Date: November 1, 2021

Previous Policy (405-2 was previously found in 465-1 until November 1, 2021)

 

For the Medically Needy Family, Child and Pregnant Woman programs, the eligibility of a minor parent under the age 18 depends on where the individual is living.

1.       When a pregnant woman under age 18 lives with her parents or stepparents, the parents’ income and assets must be deemed to her when determining her eligibility for Medically Needy Medicaid. It does not matter if the woman is emancipated. As long as she lives with her parents, the parents’ income and assets must be deemed to her when determining her eligibility.

a.       Include the unborn child in the household size.

b.       Only count the income and assets of the father of the unborn child, if the father is married to the mother of the unborn and living in the home.

Example: 16-year-old Mary is pregnant and lives with her parents. Her boyfriend is also living with this family and Mary states he is the father of the unborn child. Her boyfriend has not signed the Form 941 and they have no other child in common. The boyfriend's income or assets are not countable when determining Mary's eligibility for Family Medically Needy Medicaid.

2.       When determining eligibility for the pregnant minor's boyfriend who is the father of the unborn and living in the home, the following apply:

a.       Only count his income and assets to determine his Medicaid eligibility.

b.    The unborn can be used as a dependent child if (402-1):

                           i.      The boyfriend has acknowledged paternity by a signed Form 941; and

                         ii.      The pregnancy is in the third trimester.

3.       When determining eligibility for a child of a minor parent, the following apply:

a.       Count the income and assets of the child's parents living in the home (229-1).  

b.       Do not count the income and assets of the child's grandparents living in the home.

4.       When determining eligibility for married minor parents living with either of their parents, and both minor parents are requesting coverage, count the applicants' and the parents' income and assets to determine the minor parents' eligibility.

·       Do not count the income of the parents with whom they are living to determine eligibility if only the minor parent is requesting coverage and that individual is not the child of the parents.

Example:  Susan and Bob are married. Although they are both 16-years-old, they are emancipated through marriage. Susan and Bob live with Bob’s parents. Susan is pregnant and has filed an application for medical assistance. Bob does not want medical coverage for himself. First determine Susan's eligibility for a MAGI-based coverage group. If she does not qualify for a MAGI-based coverage group, determine her eligibility for medically needy pregnant woman based on her and Bob's income and assets. Do not count the income and assets of Bob's parents because Bob is not requesting medical coverage with Susan.
