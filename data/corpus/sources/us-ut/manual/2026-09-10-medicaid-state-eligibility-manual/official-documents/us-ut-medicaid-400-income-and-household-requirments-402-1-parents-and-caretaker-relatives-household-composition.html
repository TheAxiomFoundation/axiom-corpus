Medicaid Policy                                                                 

 

402-1 Parents and Caretaker Relatives Household Composition

Effective Date: November 1, 2021

Previous Policy (402-1 was previously found in 345 until November 1, 2021)

 

A.   PCR Household Composition

 

The PCR program follows the MAGI methodology to determine the adult’s household composition (401-1). Sections 345, 345-1 and 345-2 explain the other program requirements. The household size is used to determine if the countable income falls within the program income limit for the individual (Table VII). 

 

1.    Adults qualify for the PCR program if they meet the program requirements stated in 345, and the income requirements found in 440-1 through 440-5.

 

2.    Include the following individuals in the PCR household:

 

a.    Adults:  Both of the married parents living in the home will be included in the household.

                       i.    An unmarried couple generally cannot be part of each other’s MAGI household. They must each have their own case.

·       If the parents are unmarried and live together, they can both receive PCR if they meet the criteria in 345.

             ii.    If one parent of the unmarried couple is a tax filer and claiming the other parent as a "tax dependent" on their tax return (401), the tax dependent would be part of the tax-filer's household. However, the tax-filer would not be included in the other parent's Medicaid household.

 

b.    Children: Dependent children who are included in the MAGI household.

               i.    Children are dependent if they meet the age criteria (345-1).

             ii.    A child living with a tax filing parent, who is not claimed as a tax dependent, cannot be included in the parent’s PCR household (401-2). 

                     iii.    If unmarried parents have only one child in common, that child can qualify each of the parents for coverage.

                     iv.    Unborn children are always included in a married couple’s PCR household.

                      v.    An Unborn child can be included in a self-declared father’s household in the following situation:

1.    The woman is in the third trimester of the pregnancy, and

2.    The presumed father acknowledges paternity of the unborn by completing the Form 941.

 

c.    Non-parent caretaker relatives: Both the caretaker relative and spouse can be eligible for the PCR program if they meet the criteria in all sections included in 345. Their MAGI household will include both spouses and their tax dependents.

                       i.    The caretaker relatives may or may not be claiming the dependent, deprived relative children in their care as tax dependents.

             ii.    If the caretaker relative is not a tax filer, their household will follow the rules in 401-4.

             iii.    If the caretaker relative is not a tax filer and does not claim the children as tax dependents, the caretaker relative’s MAGI household will follow the rules in 401-3.

 

B. PCR Multi-Generational Households

 

In certain circumstances, a non-parent caretaker relative could assume primary responsibility for the dependent child's care while the child’s parent is in the home.

 

1.    Follow the MAGI methodology and the program requirements as stated in A.1.

 

2.    Allow the household to decide who has the primary responsibility for the child's care on a case by case basis.

a.    If the parent is living in the home, the presumption is that the parent is providing most of the care to the child. However, if the non-parent caretaker relative claims to be the primary caregiver, accept that statement.

b.       If the parent and the caretaker relative provide conflicting statements about who is the primary caretaker, consider the parent the primary caretaker.

c.       The same dependent child cannot qualify both a parent and a non-parent caretaker relative for PCR Medicaid.

d.    The child must still be deprived of support (345-2).

 

Example #1: Tammy Baker is a 19-year-old mother. She and her 18-month-old son, Blake, live with Tammy's mother, Sue. Tammy goes to school full-time while her mother cares for Blake. Sue is divorced and claims both Tammy and Blake as tax dependents. Sue says she is the primary caretaker for Blake. Since Blake is deprived of support and Sue does most of the care giving, Sue can be considered as the caretaker relative for Blake.
