PAS Completion

 

Overview

It is important to review the information entered before completing the PAS. Review the information to make sure:

  • It is entered correctly;

  • The scores are correct, and the comments clearly support the score;

  • The summary gives a clear description of the PAS;

  • The physical address and living arrangement are current and correct.

 

NOTE     For PAS reassessments, verify the current address and living arrangement. If this is different from what is displaying in HEAplus, contact the Benefits and Eligibility Specialist (BES) to update the demographics.

The documents and comments must support the scores given. When information appears to conflict between sources, comments must explain which sources were used to determine scores and why.

The PAS, with all comments and documents, is the legal basis for ALTCS medical eligibility decisions. Information from the PAS is shared with the ALTCS Health Plan and may be shared with federal and state auditors, the customer, the Office of Administrative Hearings, and when authorized, with other parties.

 

Things to keep in mind when completing a PAS

  • When a customer scores eligible but is experiencing an acute episode or condition that may improve, set a one-year reassessment date.

  • When information is inconsistent or questionable and the difference cannot be resolved by other information in the case file or from the caregiver, discuss the PAS with a BEM before closing it.

 

Example of How To Resolve Inconsistent Information
The customer’s medical records include neurological assessments from two different doctors:

  • The most recent assessment is from a month ago and says the customer shows a modest decline in neurological function.

  • The other assessment is almost a year old and notes a significant decline in function that impacts ADLs and a diagnosis of advanced Alzheimer’s Disease.

 
The PAS Assessor reviews the case file and asks the caregiver about the assessment results. The assessor finds out that the most recent assessment was done over the phone by a doctor who has never seen the customer in person and did not speak to the customer’s caregivers. This record also does not specify the neurological tests conducted. The older record is from a neurologist who has been treating the customer for the past five years. It provides the neurological test results and is supported by earlier records showing a steady decline in the customer’s function over the past few years.

The assessor determines that the older medical record is more reliable and uses it when completing the PAS, then adds a note about why the newer record was not used in the decision.
