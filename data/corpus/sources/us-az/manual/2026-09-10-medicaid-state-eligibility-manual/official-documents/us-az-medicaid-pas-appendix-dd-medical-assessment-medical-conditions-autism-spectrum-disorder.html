Definition

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)

ASD is a neurological and developmental condition that affects how the brain works and develops. People with ASD may have trouble with social interactions and communication and may do certain actions over and over. It is called a “spectrum disorder” because it can look very different from person to person – some people have mild challenges, while others have more serious ones. Medical professionals use the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) to explain ASD. It usually starts in childhood and lasts a lifetime. ASD can make everyday life harder than what’s normal for someone’s age and surroundings. 

There are 3 levels of ASD:

  • Level 1: Requiring support

  • Level 2: Requiring substantial support

  • Level 3: Requiring very substantial support

 

Definitions To Keep in Mind

Term

Definition

Asperger’s Disorder

Medical professionals do not use this term anymore. Asperger’s Disorder is now referred to as ASD which includes various conditions that affect average development, particularly in social and communication skills. Asperger’s Disorder is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by significant difficulties in social interaction, communication skills, and the presence of restricted or repetitive behaviors.

Childhood Disintegrative Disorder

Also called Heller’s syndrome, is a rare type of autism that medical professionals don’t fully understand. Children with this condition grow and learn normally for at least two years. Then, they suddenly lose skills they had before, like talking, playing with others, or using their hands and body.

Pervasive Developmental Disorder (PDD)

Medical professionals do not use this term anymore. PDD is now referred to as ASD which includes various conditions that affect average development, particularly in social and communication skills. PDD refers to a group of neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by significant difficulties in social interaction, communication skills, and the presence of restricted or repetitive behaviors.

 

What is Needed to Score Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) as a Major Medical Condition

An autism diagnosis must be made by a trained professional, like a psychiatrist, neurologist, licensed psychologist, nurse practitioner, or doctor who specializes in child development. The doctor or specialist will complete and submit a diagnostic evaluation regarding the individual. The evaluation will include the presence of diagnostic criteria for autism and the presence of the required number of symptoms of autism, based on current guidelines established by the American Psychiatric Association.
