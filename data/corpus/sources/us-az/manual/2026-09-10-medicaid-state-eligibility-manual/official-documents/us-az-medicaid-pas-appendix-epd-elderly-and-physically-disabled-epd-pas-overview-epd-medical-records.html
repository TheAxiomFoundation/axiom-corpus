Medical Records

 

Review all available medical records and use the information when scoring the PAS. A few examples of medical records that are useful in the PAS assessment include:

  • Discharge summary if the customer was hospitalized;

  • Consultations by specialists (for example, psychology, psychiatry, neurology, or cardiology reports);

  • Therapy notes, such as occupational therapy physical therapy, and speech therapy;

  • Nursing notes, when they address a specific incident or condition;

  • Home health care or home hospice evaluation and progress notes;

  • Laboratory results, EEG, EKG or MRI results;

  • Physician Progress Notes relating to current condition or need for long-term care;

  • MDS (Minimum Data Set) when the customer is in NF;

  • For reassessments, the long-term care case manager’s quarterly assessments.

 

Things to keep in mind when reviewing medical records

When reviewing medical records:

  • Make sure that the customer’s name is on all records and is correct;

  • For records that include the customer’s date of birth (DOB), check that it matches the DOB on file. If it does not, determine which one is correct by contacting the provider about the discrepancy.  If the provider has the incorrect DOB, but the records are for the customer, add a sentence to the summary stating the provider entered the wrong DOB, but the records are correct. If our records are incorrect contact the Benefits and Eligibility Specialist (BES) and report that the DOB in HEAplus appears to be incorrect.

  • Review the dates on medical records used to support your scores and decision. The records should be current and relevant to the customer’s condition, typically within the past six months. When older records are relevant, include the explanation in comments. For example, a customer was diagnosed with dementia years ago and that is the most current record of the diagnosis;

 

Scoring when medical records are not available or conflict with reported function

Follow the steps below when the information provided by the customer, caregiver, or representative during the PAS conflicts with the medical records, or medical records are not available:

Step

Action

1

Were medical records provided for the customer?

  • If YES, continue to step 2.

  • If NO, STOP. Score based on what the customer, caregiver or representative reported, noting any differences between what was reported by each person or between what was reported and what you observed.

2

Do the medical records support the customer’s responses?

  • If YES, STOP. Score based on the customer’s responses.

  • If NO, contact the customer, representative, or caregiver to discuss and clarify the differences. Continue to step 3.

3

Does the customer have a valid reason explaining why the medical records contradict what is being reported?

  • If YES, score based on the customer’s report and add comments to each section that has a discrepancy describing the reason the reported information was used instead of the medical records.

  • If NO, score based on the most relevant records and add comments to each section that has a discrepancy explaining why the reported information was not used. Contact your Benefits and Eligibility Manager (BEM) requesting review. The BEM determines if the PAS scores are accurate, if able. When the BEM is unable to determine the accuracy of the scores, a M.A.R.S. Inquiry is sent to PAS Policy.

 

Definitions

Term

Definition

Valid reason explaining why the medical records contradict what is being reported

Based on the PAS Assessor’s knowledge and experience, a valid reason is a logical explanation in support of why the reported information conflicts with the medical records.

Reasons may vary and could cover dozens of scenarios. Two sample reasons are given below, as examples only:

  • The customer says that he can walk “just fine” with his cane. Recent medical records state the customer cannot walk at all without weight-bearing support due to advanced Alzheimer’s. Caregiver notes say the customer tries to get up from the wheelchair daily, causing two falls in the last six months. The PAS Assessor does not find a valid reason to use the customer’s statement instead of medical records and explains why in the comments.

  • A neurological assessment from nine months ago indicates the customer’s ADLs are moderately impacted by her recently diagnosed Parkinson’s Disease. She is mainly independent but needs limited hands-on assistance with transfers in and out of chairs, bed and the shower, and stand-by assistance for mobility. The caregiver reports that the customer has declined in the past few months and now needs daily set-up and limited hands-on assistance with eating, grooming and bathing. Another neurology appointment is scheduled next week about the decline. The PAS Assessor finds the explanation a valid reason to use the statement instead of medical records, explains why in the comments, and makes a note to follow-up on the results from the customer’s neurology appointment next week.
