Medical Records

 

Overview

Medical records are a critical part of making an objective and accurate PAS decision.

Review all available medical records and use the information when scoring the PAS. A few examples of medical records that are useful in the PAS assessment include:

  • DDD/DES Person-Centered Planning Document;

  • Department of Special Education Individualized Education Program (IEP);

  • Multidisciplinary Evaluation Team (MET) record;

  • Clinical Autism Diagnostic Evaluation;

  • Consultations by specialists (for example, psychology, psychiatry, neurology, or cardiology reports);

  • Therapy notes (for example, speech, occupational, behavioral health, and physical therapy);

  • Primary Care Physician (PCP) reports.

 

Things to keep in mind when reviewing medical records

When reviewing medical records:

  • Make sure that the customer’s name is on all records and is correct.

  • For records that include the customer’s date of birth (DOB), check that it matches the DOB on file. If it does not, determine which one is correct by contacting the provider about the discrepancy. If the provider has the incorrect DOB, but the records are for the customer, add a sentence to the summary stating the provider entered the wrong DOB, but the records are correct. If our records are incorrect contact the Benefits and Eligibility Specialist (BES) and report that the DOB in HEAplus appears to be incorrect.

  • Review the dates on medical records used to support your scores and decision. The records should be current and relevant to the customer’s condition, typically within the past six months. When older records are relevant, include the explanation in comments. For example, a 50-year-old customer was diagnosed with moderate intellectual disability 35 years ago and that is the most current record of the diagnosis.

 

Scoring when medical records are not available or conflict with reported function

Follow the steps below when the information provided by the customer, caregiver, or representative during the PAS conflicts with the medical records, or medical records are not available: 

Step

Action

1

Were medical records provided for the customer?

  • If YES, continue to step 2.

  • If NO, STOP. Score based on what the caregiver or representative reported, noting any differences between what was and what you observed.

 

2

Do the medical records support the customer’s responses?

  • If YES, STOP. Score based on the customer’s responses.

  • If NO, contact the caregiver to discuss and clarify the differences. Continue to step 3.

 

3

Does the caregiver have a valid reason explaining why the medical records contradict what is being reported?

  • If YES, score based on the caregiver’s report and add comments to each section that has a discrepancy describing the reason the reported information was used instead of the medical records.

  • If NO, score based on the most relevant records and add comments to each section that has a discrepancy explaining why the reported information was not used. Contact your Benefits and Eligibility Manager (BEM) requesting review. The BEM determines if the PAS scores are accurate, if able. When the BEM is unable to determine the accuracy of the scores, a M.A.R.S. Inquiry is sent to PAS Policy.

 

 

Definitions

Term

Definition

Valid reason explaining why the medical records contradict what is being reported

Based on the PAS Assessor’s knowledge and experience, a valid reason is a logical explanation of why the reported information conflicts with the medical records.

Reasons may vary and could cover dozens of scenarios. For example:

  • The customer is a 55-year-old female diagnosed with moderate intellectual disability and lives alone. She says that she can walk “just fine” with her cane. Recent medical records state the customer is an extreme fall risk, and EMS has been contacted by neighbors four times in the past 45 days. The PAS Assessor contacts the customer to ask about the falls reported in the records and the customer says she does not remember falling. The PAS Assessor does not find a valid reason to use the customer’s statement instead of medical records and explains why in the comments.

  • A Clinical Autism Diagnostic Evaluation from six months ago indicates the customer was using a few words and making frequent eye contact with his parents. The customer’s mother reports that when her child was 12 months old, he was saying a few words and looking at her often. She explained that at 17 months of age he became completely non-verbal and no longer makes eye contact with anyone. The mother is extremely concerned and has scheduled an appointment with the licensed psychologist who evaluated her son six months ago. The PAS Assessor finds the explanation a valid reason to use the statement instead of medical records, explains why in the comments.
