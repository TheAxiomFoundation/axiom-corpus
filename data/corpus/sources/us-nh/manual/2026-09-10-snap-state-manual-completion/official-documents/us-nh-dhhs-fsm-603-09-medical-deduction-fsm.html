Medical Deduction (FSM)

SR 25-30 Dated 12/25

Previous Policy

_____________________________________________________________

SNAP households containing an elderly or disabled member (target population) are entitled to a standard medical deduction of $135 per month if the household’s monthly unreimbursed medical costs exceed $35 but are less than or equal to $135. If the unreimbursed medical costs minus $35 are more than $135, the household is entitled to the verified actual costs.

 

Example: An individual pays $175 in unreimbursed medical expenses each month ($175 - $35 = $140). The unreimbursed medical expenses are more than $135, therefore the household is entitled to the verified actual costs of $175.

 

Only those expenses for the target individual in the assistance group are allowed.

 

Care must be provided by a licensed practitioner or qualified health professional and should be treated in the following ways:

·        Reasonable estimates of anticipated unreimbursed medical expenses shall be averaged over the certification period.

·        Verification or reporting of anticipated expenses as they occur during the certification period are not required.

·        Verified one-time costs shall be treated as a one-month deduction or averaged over the certification period, as the individual indicates.

Exception: For households certified for 36 months that report a one-time medical expense incurred during the first 12 months of the certification period, give the household the option of deducting the expense for one month, averaging the expense over the remainder of the first 12 months of the certification period, or averaging the expense over the remaining months in the certification period.

One-time expenses reported in the second 12 months of the certification period will be deducted for one month, averaged over the remainder of the second 12 months of the certification period, or averaged over the remaining months in the certification period, at the household’s option.

One-time expenses reported after the 24th month of the certification period will be deducted in one month or averaged over the remaining months in the certification period, at the household’s option.

·        Unanticipated changes reported by the household or sources outside the household that occur during the certification period, only if such changes are verified upon receipt, and without contacting the household.

 

Medical deductions include, but are not limited to:

·       dentures, hearing aids, prosthetics

·       eyeglasses/contact lenses

·       lodging to obtain medical care

·       medical and dental care

·       hospitalization

·       outpatient care

·       nursing care

·       medications (prescription and over the counter) when prescribed as part of a medical treatment plan

·       medical supplies and equipment, including sick room equipment, (rented or purchased) or other prescribed equipment

·       psychotherapy

·       rehabilitation services

·       nursing facility care paid by an assistance group member for a target individual who was a member of the assistance group immediately prior to entering the hospital or nursing facility recognized by the state.

·       monthly installments for verified payment plans or loan agreements for medical bills for the target individual.

·       securing and maintaining a prescribed service animal (most commonly a dog) that is specially trained to perform a function the individual cannot readily perform on their own, or that assists the individual with the medical issue for which the animal is prescribed.  

o    When questionable, verification in the form of a prescription or signed and dated statement from the healthcare provider must be provided. 

o    Specific types of training, credentials, and/or certifications are not required; verification of training may be in the form of self-attestation (written or verbal) and must be documented in case comments.

o    Allowable expenses include the cost to obtain the service animal, special equipment such as halters or coats that identify the animal as a service animal, food and veterinary care. Verification of allowable expenses, (receipts) are required.

o    Other items such as toys, clothing that does not identify the service animal, dishes, grooming supplies and services, bedding, etc. are not allowable expenses.

o    Expenses related to a companion or emotional support animal are not allowable, even if the companion or support animal has been recommended by a healthcare professional.

·       transportation costs, including, but not limited to, transportation costs to and from doctor’s appointments, testing, treatment, and pharmacies/drug stores to pick up medication, including both prescription and over-the-counter medicine, when it’s part of a medical treatment plan. The allowable transportation cost for a private vehicle is determined by multiplying the number of miles traveled for medical needs by the federal mileage rate for medical travel, which is determined annually by the IRS (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/standard-mileage-rates). If the individual uses public transportation (bus, taxi, Uber, etc.), the actual cost is allowable.

Example 1: A target individual’s doctor conducts lab work and determines that the individual needs to start taking an over-the-counter supplement to combat tiredness. The individual takes a taxi to pharmacy/drug store to pick up the supplement. The cost of the taxi ride will count as a medical expense.

Example 2: If a target individual’s friend recommends that the individual take a potassium supplement to combat tiredness, this trip to the pharmacy/drug store would not count as a medical deduction.

·        shipping costs and delivery fees of medical supplies, treatments and medications, including both prescription and over-the-counter medicine when prescribed by a licensed practitioner or qualified health professional as part of a medical treatment plan.

Example 1: A target individual uses an online pharmacy service and receives their prescriptions through the mail each month.  Some of the medications prescribed are covered by Medicaid, and some are not. The individual pays $29.00 out of pocket for medications and $6.99 per month for shipping. The cost of the medications that are not covered by Medicaid, plus the shipping cost total $35.99. The individual is entitled to the standard medical deduction of $135.00 per month because their monthly unreimbursed medical expenses exceed $35.00.

Example 2: A target individual has their prescriptions filled at a local pharmacy. The individual does not drive and therefore has their prescriptions delivered by the pharmacy. The pharmacy charges a $4.99 fee for delivery. The individual pay’s $35.00 out of pocket each month for prescriptions plus $4.99 for delivery totaling $39.99. The individual is entitled to the standard medical deduction of $135.00 because their monthly unreimbursed medical expenses exceed $35.

Note: Shipping/mailing costs and delivery fees are akin to transportation costs of obtaining medical services and treatment when prescribed as part of a medical treatment plan. 

·       costs of maintaining an attendant, homemaker, home health aid, housekeeper, or child care service if necessary, due to the target individual’s age, physical or mental health, or illness.

o    An amount equal to the one person benefit allotment shall be deducted if the household provides the majority of the attendant's meals.

o    If the attendant care costs qualify under both the medical deduction policy and the dependent care deduction policy, the cost is treated as a medical expense.

Exception: If the household is reimbursed for attendant care costs through the VA Aid and Attendance Allowance, the reimbursement must be manually subtracted from the total attendant care cost to determine the actual cost to the individual.

●    cost of health or hospitalization insurances (including deductible and cost-sharing of expenses).

 

Exceptions:

·        Policies that pay lump sum benefits for death or dismemberment or pay expenses while one is disabled are excluded.

·        The costs of medical marijuana may not be used as a medical deduction.

 

References: He-W 742.01; He-W 758.01; 7 CFR 273.9(d)(3); 7 USC 2014(e)(5)(C); 7 CFR 273.2(f)(4)(i); 7 CFR 273.10(d)(3)
