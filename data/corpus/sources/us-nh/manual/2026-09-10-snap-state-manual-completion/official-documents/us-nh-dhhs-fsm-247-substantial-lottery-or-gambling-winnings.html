247 SUBSTANTIAL LOTTERY OR GAMBLING WINNINGS  (FSM)

SR 26-12 Dated 03/26

Previous Policy

_____________________________________________________________

Substantial lottery or gambling winnings are defined as a cash prize which is equal to or greater than the SNAP resource limit for elderly or disabled households (currently $4,500) won in a single game, before taxes or other amounts are withheld. If multiple individuals shared in the purchase of a ticket, hand, or similar bet, then only the portion of the winnings allocated to the member of the SNAP household will be counted toward the eligibility determination.

Households with substantial lottery or gambling winnings shall immediately lose eligibility for SNAP benefits, even if the household immediately spends or loses a substantial portion of the winnings and the remaining winnings are below the resource limit for elderly and disabled households.

Substantial lottery or gambling winnings received prior to an initial application do not affect eligibility. Only consider winnings received during the certification period or won at the time of the renewal.

Households disqualified for substantial lottery or gambling winnings are not permanently disqualified. These households may apply for SNAP benefits at any time. The next time such a household reapplies and is certified for SNAP after losing eligibility under this rule, the household cannot be considered categorically eligible or expanded categorically eligible. The households disqualified for substantial winnings remain ineligible until they meet the resource and income eligibility requirements under regular SNAP rules.

This requirement is not permanent. It only applies the first time the household is certified following the loss of eligibility. 

 

References: 7 CFR 273.11(r)
