ALIEN GROUP

DESCRIPTION

Amerasian Immigrant

Amerasian children born to U.S. military personnel and Asian nationals during the Korean and Vietnam Wars. The Amerasian Act of 1982 allowed individuals born in Korea, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, or Thailand between 1951 and 1982, and fathered by a U.S. citizen, to immigrate and become permanent residents. In 1987, Congress expanded support through the Amerasian Homecoming Act, which applied specifically to Vietnamese Amerasians born between 1962 and 1976 and living in Vietnam in 1988. This later law also permitted their mothers and immediate family members to relocate to the United States, and unlike the original program, it has no end date.

Asylee

An alien in the United States or at a port of entry who is unable or unwilling to return to their country of nationality, or to seek the protection of that country because of persecution or a well-founded fear of persecution. Persecution or the fear of persecution must be based on religion, nationality, membership in a particular social group or political opinion.

Battered aliens

Battered or subject to extreme cruelty as defined in Public Law 104-193, section 408(7)( c).In certain circumstances, eligibility may extend to a battered alien spouse or child, an alien parent of a battered child, or an alien child of a battered parent with a pending petition.

Certain American Indians born abroad

American Indians born in Canada living in the United States (U.S.) under §289 of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) or alien members of a federally recognized Indian tribe under §4(e) of the Indian Self-Determination and Education Assistance Act.

Compacts of Free Association (COFA) citizens of the Federated States of Micronesia, the Republic of the Marshall Islands, and the Republic of Palau.

Individuals who lawfully reside in the U.S. in accordance with section 141 of the Compacts of Free Association between the Government of the United States and the Governments of the Federated States of Micronesia, the Republic of the Marshall Islands, and the Republic of Palau.

Conditional entrants

Granted conditional entry under §203(a)(7)- (8 USC 1153) of the INA as in effect before 04/01/80.

Cuban or Haitian entrants

Cuban or Haitian entrant under §501(e) of the Refugee Education Assistance Act of 1980.

Deportation (or removal) withheld

Deportation is being withheld under section 243(h) (8 USC 1253) of the INA as in effect before 04/01/97, or removal is withheld under §241(b)(3) of the INA.

Hmong or Highland Laotian tribal members

An individual lawfully residing in the U.S. who was a member of a Hmong or Highland Laotian tribe that rendered assistance to U.S. personnel by taking part in a military or rescue operation during the Vietnam era (Aug. 5, 1964 – May 7, 1975). This category includes the spouse (or un-remarried surviving spouse) or unmarried dependent children of these individuals.

Individuals granted asylum

Granted asylum under section 208(b) (8 USC 1158) of the INA.

Iraqi and Afghan special immigrants (SIV)

 

Special immigrant status under section 101(a)(27) of the INA may be granted to Iraqi and Afghan nationals who have worked on behalf of the U.S. government in Iraq or Afghanistan. The Department of Defense Appropriations Act of 2010, PL 111-118, section 8120 enacted on Dec. 19, 2009, provides that SIVs are eligible for all benefits to the same extent and the same time period as refugees.

Lawful Permanent resident (LPR)

LPRs, also known as “Green Card” holders, are aliens who are lawfully authorized to live permanently in the United States. This category also includes “Amerasian immigrants” as defined under section 584 of the Foreign Operations, Export Financing and Related Programs Appropriations Act of 1988.

Military connection

An individual who is lawfully residing in a State and is on active duty in the military (excluding National Guard) or is an honorably discharged veteran whose discharge is not because of immigration status (includes spouse, surviving spouse if not married, and unmarried dependent children). A discharge “Under Honorable Conditions”, which is not the same as an honorable discharge, does not meet this requirement

Non- Citizen U.S National

Unique legal category that describes individuals who are not U.S. citizens but owes permanent allegiance to the United States per 8 USC 1101(a)(22)(B).

Parolees

Paroled into the U.S. under section 212(d)(5)- (8 USC 1182) of the INA for a period of at least 1 year.

Refugees

Refugees admitted to the U.S. under section 207 ( 8 USC 1157) of the INA

Victims of a severe form of trafficking in person and certain family members

Victims under the Trafficking Victims Protection Act of 2000 and family members granted derivative nonimmigrant status.

 

References: 7 CFR 273.4(a)(2)-(6); 8 USC 1611-1613, 1621, & 1641; 62 Federal Register (FR) 61348-61349 (Nov. 17, 1997)
