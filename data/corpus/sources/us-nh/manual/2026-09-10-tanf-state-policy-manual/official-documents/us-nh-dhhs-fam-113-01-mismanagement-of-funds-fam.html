113.01 Mismanagement of Funds (FAM)

SR 97-27 Dated 08/97

Previous Policy

____________________________________________________________

When a recipient persistently mismanages the financial assistance payment, causing the funds to not be used in the best interest of the recipient or of the dependent children, it is an indication that a protective payee may need to be utilized.

 

Evidence of mismanagement includes, but not limited to, any of the following:

•       Recurring inability to plan for and meet a month’s needs;

•       Recurring failure to meet obligations for shelter and food;

•       Repeated evictions or incurrence of debts with attachments against non-assistance income; or

•       Recurring incidents of children not being properly fed or clothed, and their health and safety being threatened as a result.

 

Note:  Physical or mental incapacity alone is not a valid reason for assigning a protective payee.

 

Exception: The following are not valid reasons for protective payments:

•       Lack of funds to meet emergencies such as a sudden decrease in income or a sudden illness;

•       Expenses for necessary bills exceed the individual’s grant and other income; or

•       The individual is withholding payment as a reasonable exercise of consumer rights when there is a legitimate dispute about an agreement.

 

A review is conducted as frequently as needed based on the individual’s circumstances or at minimum every 12 months, to determine the continued need for a protective payee, and whether the protective payee is meeting the responsibilities.

 

Terminate protective payments when:

•       The individual is able to manage funds; or

•       A guardian or other legal representation is obtained.

 

 

References: He-W 686.01; RSA 167:5; RSA 167:26; RSA 167:83,II(g-h); 45 CFR 234.60
