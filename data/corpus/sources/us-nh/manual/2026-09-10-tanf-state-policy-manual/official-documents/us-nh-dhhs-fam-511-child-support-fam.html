Direct Child Support

Child support payment(s) made by the responsible parent directly to a household for the maintenance of a child, including any amount diverted from court ordered support to a third party when the court has not ordered the diversion. Child support payments are considered the income of the child.

Treatment: Unearned Income

 

Example: Megan is court-ordered to pay $100 per week in child support for her daughter Kate. Megan gives Kate’s father $50 each week and pays the other $50 directly to Kate’s daycare. The full $100 counts as unearned income for Kate because the court did not order the diversion to the daycare.

 

 

Retained Child Support

Any portion of child support made payable to—and retained by—the state:

Treatment: Excluded Income for grant computation in the benefits test. It is not excluded from the eligibility test.

 

If child support payments are made on behalf of more than one child, prorate the payment among the children to determine an amount for each (unless otherwise stipulated by court or administrative order). This includes any amount diverted from court-ordered support (or other binding agreement) to a third party to pay the household’s expenses when the court has not ordered the diversion. If spousal support is included in the same payment with child support, a prorated share is considered to be the spouse’s.

 

Example: Kenny sends his ex-wife Lisa $400 each month to support their two children, Max and Maggie. This amount is prorated among the children, so Max and Maggie each have $200 in countable unearned income each month.

 

References: He-W 654.09; 45 CFR 233.20(a)(3)(v)
