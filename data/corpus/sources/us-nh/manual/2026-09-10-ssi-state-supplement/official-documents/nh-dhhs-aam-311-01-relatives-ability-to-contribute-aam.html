311.01 Relative's Ability to Contribute (AAM)

SR 26-03 Dated 03/26

Previous Policy

__________________________________________________________________

To evaluate whether a liable relative is able to contribute to the support of an applicant or recipient of financial assistance, consider the following:

 

•       Number of Legal Dependents

The number of legal dependents claimed by the liable relative for IRS purposes

Note: the applicant or recipient is not included as a legal dependent, even if residing with the relative.

 

•       Gross Annual Income

If self-employed, deduct cost of doing business.

 

•       Allowable Deductions

Certain expenses may be deducted from gross annual income before assessing liability or contribution amount. These may include:

o    Unreimbursed or unreimbursable medical costs

o    Educational expenses

o    Court-ordered support or alimony payments

o    Repayments, including garnishments, or prior indebtedness

o    Business related costs for self-employed individuals

o    Exceptional expenses that affect health, safety, or prevent homelessness

Examples include : home repairs, back taxes, transportation for medical services; expenses related to natural disasters such as floods, fires, earthquakes and hurricanes; and expenses resulting from manmade events such as theft of clothing, food or money, eviction, or loss of an owned residence.

Note: All exceptional expenses require written approval from a supervisor.

The liable relative’s required contribution is calculated by comparing their gross annual income minus any approved expenses to the amounts listed in the table:

·         If their adjusted gross income is less than or equal to the table amount, no contribution is required.

·         If their adjusted gross income is greater than the table amount, they must contribute at least 10% of the amount exceeding the table value.
